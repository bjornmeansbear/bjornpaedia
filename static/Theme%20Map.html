This is a working map of the recurring clusters in this wiki.

Core clusters

1. Sustainability and systems

  • climate, ecology, circularity, materials, energy, scale, planetary limits
  • This is the largest umbrella: how design, culture, and everyday life relate to living systems.
  • Good entry points: Define: Sustainability, Waste Equals Food, Climate Solutions 101

2. Design as practice and worldview

  • design research, aesthetics, visual language, typography, form/content/context, Bauhaus, meaning-making
  • This cluster treats design as a way of seeing and shaping the world, not just a visual discipline.
  • Good entry points: What is Design Research?, Thoughts on Friz, Form <> Content <> Context

3. Open source, commons, and digital culture

  • F/LOSS, software, tools, collaboration, web culture, knowledge sharing
  • These pieces connect design to shared infrastructure, authorship, and collective making.
  • Good entry points: F/LOSS, Designers Can Open Source, The Libre Designer

4. Ethics, politics, and critique

  • principles, values, power, equity, justice, responsibility, social change
  • This layer asks what design is for, and what kinds of worlds it reinforces or resists.
  • Good entry points: An Explanation, Equity of Design Software, The Audre Lorde Questionnaire to Oneself

5. Writing, reflection, and personal practice

  • journaling, notes, prompts, quotes, thinking in public, the essay form
  • A lot of the archive is not just argument; it is also practice, observation, and self-education.
  • Good entry points: Writing, The Sustainabilitist Principles, What is SGD? (020210414)

6. Materials, making, and the material world

  • repair, waste, circular economy, objects, production, built systems
  • This cluster helps connect abstract ideas to concrete lifeworlds and physical consequences.
  • Good entry points: Re-thinking Plastics: Towards a Recycular Economy, Silvopasture, Waste Equals Food

7. Typography, signs, and visual languages

  • typefaces, symbols, semiotics, signage, meaning in form
  • A recurring thread in the corpus is that visual language is not neutral; it carries values and assumptions.
  • Good entry points: Thoughts on Friz, Signs Signaling Sustainability, The visual conventions of modernism are not timeless truths

    Bjørnpaedia

    Bjørnpaedia is Kristian Bjørnard's running archive of essays, questions, notes, research, and half-formed ideas on sustainability, circular design, open-source practice (open sourcery?), and design education (and more). What it is not is a perfect portfolio of finished conclusions. The collection is full of thinking out loud, looking for interconnection, and circling recurring questions: what does it mean to design for the welfare of all life, why climate change is inseparable from social and cultural change, what open access and transparency owe to design practice, and how to teach the next generation of designers to interrogate their own work rather than default to easy, greenwashed answers. A working design philosophy built over time.
