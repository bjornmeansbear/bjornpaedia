Thoughts on Friz

The ubiquity of a certain typeface is an interesting thing. Maybe it’s just that once you become attuned to something you see it everywhere? Like when you get a new car, and then suddenly you see that same car everywhere — they were really always there, but you just hadn’t been “trained” to notice them yet.

Something like happened to me recently. A colleague turned me onto the typeface “Friz Quadrata” — I was familiar with it before, I knew it as the type from the famous (infamous?) Black Flag logo, but I hadn’t ever used it before. So, I decided to try it out on something… All of a sudden I see it all over the place. Things I’ve looked at thousands of times before, suddenly, there is Friz, popping suddenly out of seemingly nowhere.

Safeway uses it on interior signage. My favorite Aquavit uses it as the main logotype. Jif peanut butter uses it for creamy/extra crunchy classification (it’s horribly squished in the extra crunchy use case). Heck, even KFC suddenly shows up on my radar now — I can’t not see the KFC in all its Friz glory.

You need to understand, or at least appreciate, something to notice it. One you get something’s nuances or charms, then you start seeing it everywhere.

This is an interesting thought from a design perspective. In deciding to use one typeface for a lot of design work I inadvertently trained myself to see its unique forms and characters. Now I see them in places I just didn’t notice them before. Through continued observation I’m more attuned to that specific font’s forms. All the type I use a lot is like this… suddenly wham! there’s Open Sans, or there’s League Gothic… all it takes is an E or an A or lowercase G and you suddenly see the forest for the trees.

I’ve had similar experiences with other typefaces in the past. Gotham was like that, I got hooked on it, then couldn’t help but notice it in use everywhere. I’ve also noticed this phenomenon when I decide that I don’t like something. It is interestingly even easier to spot things I hate rather than things I like. I don’t really enjoy Rotis for example — none of the cuts of it — and so I spot it everywhere it is used (often for bank identities for some reason).

Anyway. I’m sure you’ve had this experience with a car model or book or something similar. I just happen to look at type more than almost anything else all day, so it’s what I end up spotting in weird places out in the wild.
