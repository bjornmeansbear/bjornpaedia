Bioregional Design: Rethinking Furniture Production Through Local Systems and the Circular Economy

This chapter examines how Sustainable Design can be revolutionized through bioregional thinking and circular economy principles, using furniture production as a case study. The current contemporary approach to manufacturing often follows a linear, globalized model that disconnects production from local resources and knowledge. The main project in the chapter, the Oka chair ([okaterra.com](https://www.okaterra.com/)), proposes a paradigm shift that emphasizes designing within one’s bioregions, utilizing local ecological knowledge, and embracing circularity.

Central to this approach is the concept that while design *ideas* can scale globally, production should remain local and responsive to regional conditions and constraints. This creates distinct variations in products based on available materials and local expertise — for instance, a chair produced in one bioregion will necessarily differ from one made in another, reflecting each area's unique ecological and cultural context. The chapter explores how sustainability initiatives can revitalize local material sourcing and manufacturing traditions, where product forms evolve organically from regionally available materials, tools, and production systems rather than forcing materials to conform to predetermined designs.

The analysis emphasizes the importance of maintaining circular systems within communities, encompassing not just materials but also knowledge, resources, and capital. Using the Okaterra chair design case study, as well as some other experiments from larger companies like Emeco, we examine how these principles can be applied to larger-scale projects and challenge conventional sustainable design practices.

The Okaterra project questions the prevalent assumption that all production must scale globally and instead proposes a model where while the design thinking can be universal, the production remains intentionally local and environmentally conscious. This approach promotes environmental sustainability while strengthening local economies and preserving regional traditions, and offers spaces of innovation when dealing with constraints imposed by one’s bioregion.
