Defining Sustainable Design.

but first do we have to define design?

Design is the intentional creation of plans or conceptions for various products, systems, or environments. It involves a deep understanding of the needs, desires, and constraints of users, as well as the technical and aesthetic considerations of the design process. Design can range from the creation of physical objects to the development of complex systems and experiences. (need to utilize the philosophy of design, way of design, and maybe some of the things my students referned in GFD for definitions?)

Historically, sustainability has often meant doing things the same, but with less bad materials. At this point in our efforts, we have to do better than that. Sustainable design is about creating products, systems, and environments that not only minimize harm but actively contribute to the well-being of the planet and its inhabitants. At its core, sustainable design is guided by a simple yet powerful principle: design objects so that we leave things better than we found them. This concept goes beyond merely reducing environmental impact; it aims to improve conditions for future generations.

Get into John Ehrenfeld and Bruce Mau's definitions; that leads us from sustainability to the regeneration and then finally circularity.

So that is where perhaps some of these other terms come in — circularity and regeneration.

So no we define regenerative design; how it goes PAST sustainability; and the circularity is a way to more clearly articulate the kinds of thkning and systems that could lead us to regeneration as design's main underlying framework…

  • Victor Papanek?
    • Victor Papanek: A pioneer in sustainable design, Papanek advocated for design that serves human needs while minimizing environmental impact. His book, Design for the Real World, remains a seminal work in the field.
  • R. Buckminster Fuller?
    • R. Buckminster Fuller: Fuller’s concept of "Synergetics" emphasized the efficient use of resources and the interconnectedness of systems. His geodesic domes are iconic examples of sustainable design. Spaceship Earth as an example of how we need to start thinking like captains and astronauts — there is no resupply mission, how to be better stewards of all our resources?)
  • Cradle to Cradle?
    • Cradle to Cradle: This design framework, popularized by William McDonough and Michael Braungart, emphasizes the creation of products that are fully recyclable or biodegradable, with no waste or pollution.
